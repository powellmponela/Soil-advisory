Workflow overview

The platform is designed as a reproducible evidence chain. Raw NSAF maize trial observations are harmonized first; observed nutrient response and nitrogen-use efficiency are then characterized; empirically supported fertilizer-rate alternatives are identified; spatial models extend those relationships only to model-supported pixels; and validated pixel products are published to the web. The dashboard is therefore a delivery layer rather than a place where agronomic models are recalculated.

Raw NSAF trials→Harmonized treatment data→Trial response→N target / efficiency→Spatial extrapolation→Web GIS products→Advisory · Research
Public Advisory: trial-supported and spatial-model outputs only. QUEFTS is excluded from the public recommendation layer.
Research workspace: trial diagnostics, response curves, QUEFTS, DSM/spatial modelling, scenario comparison, model diagnostics and methodology.

Stage 0

Data ingestion and harmonization

The pipeline starts with the multi-year NSAF maize trial files for 2017–2019. These files contain site, district, treatment, nutrient-rate, variety and yield observations collected under different trial designs. Stage 0 standardizes these source structures into a common analytical format, cleans treatment labels, harmonizes units, retains provenance and prepares spatial identifiers for later mapping.

Input dataRaw NSAF maize trial files under Data/NSAF Crops Trial Data/Maize; treatment metadata; site and coordinate fields; boundary and ecological-zone layers where needed.
Main scriptsscripts/0a_nsaf_maize_ingest.py
scripts/0b_aez_spatial_join_to_maize.py
scripts/0c_nsaf_data_legacy.py
Main outputsHarmonized trial-level data, provenance tables, merged treatment summaries and standardized site/year/treatment records used by all later stages.
Examples of downstream harmonized products: merged_maize_trials_carob_like.csv maize_trials_merged_carob_summary.xlsx summary_maize_by_year_site_treatment.csv

Stage 1 establishes the yield floor under no fertilizer and characterizes spatial variability in indigenous nutrient supply. The no-input reference provides the baseline against which nutrient additions and management alternatives are interpreted.

Stage 2

Nutrient omission and 0PK reference

This stage moves from the no-input baseline to nutrient-omission contrasts. The treatment matrix separates nitrogen, phosphorus and potassium limitations using 0PK, N0K, NP0 and full NPK treatments. These contrasts quantify nutrient-specific yield response before any spatial extrapolation is attempted.

Input dataStage 0 harmonized treatment data plus omission-treatment labels and comparator definitions.

OutputsYield response to N, P and K; omission-treatment summaries; comparative figures; multi-year omission maps.
Stage 3

Farmer practice and management alternatives

Stage 3 introduces management alternatives that are closer to farm practice, including FYM + N60 and other source/timing options available in the NSAF experiments. The objective is to determine whether management changes can sustain yield while reducing mineral-N demand or improving nitrogen-use efficiency.

Stage 4

Government recommendation and comparator evidence

The government-recommended fertilizer treatment provides an operational benchmark against which lower-N or alternative management strategies are evaluated. Stage 4 therefore anchors later “same-yield” and N-reduction comparisons to a recognizable reference treatment.

Input dataGovernment recommendation treatment, 0PK baseline, farmer-practice treatments and related NSAF demo/trial observations.
Main scriptsscripts/04_stage4_government_N120.py

OutputsComparator tables for yield, relative yield, N rate, AE-N and strategy performance relative to the government recommendation.

Stage 5

N-rate adjustment and trial-supported target setting

Stage 5 uses observed rate-response evidence—especially the multi-rate 2017 sequence—to determine how far N can be increased or reduced before marginal yield response becomes small. This is the core stage for deriving a trial-supported N target rather than relying on a theoretical nutrient requirement.

Stage 6

Efficiency alternatives and strategy consolidation

Stage 6 consolidates the evidence across trial years and management strategies. It focuses on whether alternative N management can preserve agronomic performance while improving AE-N/PFP-N or reducing mineral-N demand. This is also where strategy-specific evidence is prepared for model training.

Stage 7

Model training and web-GIS preparation

The consolidated response evidence is translated into a predictive model that can be applied to covariate-supported locations. The training data link observed trial response to environmental and spatial covariates, while QA checks track site coverage and model support. A parallel publication script prepares map-ready data structures.

Final architecture

NSAF raw data ↓ Stage 0: ingest + harmonize ↓ Stages 1–2: baseline + omission response ↓ Stages 3–4: farmer practice + government comparator ↓ Stage 5: trial-supported N-rate target ↓ Stage 6: efficiency alternatives + consolidated training evidence ↓ Stage 7: predictive spatial modelling ↓ Stage 8: static maps + advisory_pixels.* publication ↓ GitHub main → Vercel ↓ Advisory | Research

Interpretation principle

The platform separates evidence generation from public delivery. NSAF field trials establish observed nutrient response and efficient N-rate ranges. Spatial modelling then extends those relationships to supported pixels using DSM and other covariates cautiously. The public Advisory reports the modelled, trial-supported result; QUEFTS and unconstrained target-yield scenarios remain in the Research workspace for diagnostics and method development.
